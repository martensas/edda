\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Cadman’s Hymn} is a short Old English poem found in numerous recensions of Bede’s English history, attributed to the illiterate shepherd Cadman (OE \emph{Cædmon}).  It employs traditional Germanic poetic formulae and likely draws on earlier, now-lost pagan compositions.

The hymn has two rhetorical parts.  Lines 1--4 serve as an introductory prayer and declaration of purpose, after which ll.~5--9 describe the creation of the earth on behalf of mankind, first of the Heaven (5--6) and then of Middenyard and the Earth (7--9).

\subsection{Orthography}

The pres. ed. marks etymologically long unstressed vowels; this is on based on Kaluza’s law in the younger \Beowulf, which shows that such likely survived into the 8th century.
