\section{The Knosping Galder}\setBookCode{Chnospinci}

\begin{flushright}%
\textbf{Dating:} C9th--10th

\textbf{Meter:} Germanic alliterative meter
\end{flushright}

\subsection{Introduction}

The \textbf{Knosping galder} is a short OHG galder for blessing a house.

\subsubsection{Textual preservation and transmission}

The galder is found in Ms. Car. C 176, presently in Zürich.  It is found on fol.~154r amidst a number of magical charms in Latin.  It is given the title \emph{Ad signandum domum contra diabolum} ‘To bless a house against devils’.

\subsubsection{Interpretation}

The galder begins by addressing a “wight”, recognising it as such.  This “wight” appears to be a malevolent spirit believed to inhabit the house.  The recognition of the wight is the equivalent of the naming of it, a common concept in exorcism; the naming of the dæmonic spirit brings it out into the open, thereby making it vulnerable to the effects of the galderer.

The titular \emph{chnospinci} ‘Knosping’ serves to drive away the now-exposed wight; the word itself appears to be a magic word and lacks no clear meaning.  Metrically, it appears that the suffix \emph{-pinci} has secondary stress; the stress suggests the segmentation \emph{chnos-pinci}, though that does not help much with the etymology.

\subsubsection{Text}

\bvg\bva \alst{W}ola, \alst{w}iht, taz tú \alst{w}ęist, \hld\ taz tu \alst{w}iht hęizist, &
taz tú ne węist noch ne \alst{ch}anst \hld\ cheden \alst{ch}nos-pinci.\eva

\bvb Lo, wight, thou knowest that thou art called a wight, \\
that thou neither knowest nor canst say [the word] ‘Knosping’.\evb\evg
